# CAOCAP Product Vision

## Purpose

CAOCAP exists to help complete beginners move from uncertainty about digital work to an informed direction, demonstrated skills, and greater independence.

The product is intended to make the path from learning to earning understandable and actionable without promising a particular income or employment outcome.

## Target audience

CAOCAP is for people who want to pursue digital work but do not yet know:

- which skills exist or what practising them feels like;
- which direction fits their interests, circumstances, and developing abilities;
- how learning activities connect to useful work; or
- how to demonstrate their abilities to other people.

The initial experience prioritizes complete beginners. It assumes curiosity and willingness to practise, not prior technical knowledge.

## Problem

Before someone can learn a skill, they need to choose a direction. Beginners are often asked to make that choice from course descriptions, career claims, or abstract lists of skills. Those sources do not show what the work actually involves or whether the learner enjoys doing it.

Even after choosing a skill, learners can struggle to turn disconnected lessons into practical capability. They may lack feedback, evidence of progress, portfolio material, and a clear bridge between practice and paid opportunities.

## Product approach

CAOCAP helps learners make decisions through experience. Learners explore digital skills by completing small guided tasks, reflecting on the experience, and comparing possible directions.

After a learner chooses a direction, an AI mentor supports a progression of practical projects. The mentor provides contextual guidance and feedback while keeping the learner responsible for decisions and work. Completed projects become evidence of developing capability and potential portfolio material.

CAOCAP then helps the learner understand how those capabilities may be presented and applied in realistic paid-work contexts. It prepares learners to pursue opportunities; it does not guarantee that opportunities or income will result.

## Intended learner journey

1. **Explore:** Understand several digital skills through clear explanations and small examples.
2. **Try:** Complete representative guided tasks instead of choosing from descriptions alone.
3. **Choose:** Compare experiences and select a direction, with the freedom to reconsider it later.
4. **Build:** Develop capability through increasingly meaningful projects and feedback.
5. **Demonstrate:** Turn suitable completed work into evidence and portfolio material.
6. **Prepare:** Learn how to describe, package, and apply the skill in paid-opportunity contexts.

The journey is directional rather than rigid. Learners may revisit earlier stages as their interests, abilities, or circumstances change.

## Product principles

### Experience before commitment

Learners should be able to try representative work before committing significant time to a direction.

### Practice produces evidence

Learning activities should result in observable work, feedback, or demonstrated progress—not completion markers alone.

### Guidance preserves agency

The AI mentor should explain, prompt, and provide feedback without removing the learner's ownership of choices or work.

### Beginner clarity

Language, navigation, and feedback should be understandable without assumed industry knowledge.

### Progress can change direction

A learner's chosen path should be revisable. Changing direction is treated as informed learning, not failure.

### Preparation without promises

CAOCAP should connect learning to realistic opportunity contexts while avoiding guarantees about employment or income.

### Trust through transparency

Learners should understand when AI is involved, what information is used, and the limits of automated guidance.
